Oneiromancy UX Review

The app now shows real league data and has a voice nobody else has. What it lacks is a point of view about the manager's week. Every screen shows celestial scores; almost none of them end in a decision. This review proposes organizing the app around the four calls a Sleeper manager makes, with the stars as a lens you can turn up or down.

Reviewed live in Chromium at 1440×900 and 390×844 as McTwerkface (ASStrologians, Toxic Trash Talkers, 12-team dynasty superflex). Every number below is from that session.

01 · The job

A manager's week is four questions

The app's goal is to help a Sleeper manager acquire and position players using celestial standings. In season that means four recurring calls. Today the answers are spread across five tabs, and none of the tabs ends in one.

PositioningWho do I start?Now: a start/sit pair that compares a starter with a same-position bench player (Taylor 18.9 over Braelon Allen 15.3).Missing: a lineup check across all slots.
AcquiringWho do I add?Now: a 196-row board plus 5 waiver cards on another tab, ranked by a draft-era "DraftScore".Missing: need-aware picks and a path to Sleeper.
AcquiringWho do I trade with?Now: 12 roster cards with 180 rows, and Divine Swaps, which is empty in live leagues.Missing: any trade framing at all.
ContextWhere do I stand?Now: Standings tab (added this week), bye matrix, matchup history.Mostly there; needs to lead, not trail.
02 · Findings

Seventeen findings, worst first

P1 blocks the app's purpose. P2 costs trust or time on every visit. P3 is polish. Each finding names where it shows up, what I saw, and the change.

Showing 17 findings

P1

Nothing answers "what should I do this week"

Oneiromancy tab (home)

Home opens on "Ideal Team & League Telemetry": a matchup card, four stat tiles, a radar chart and an aspect reader. Actions live on Matchups, Player Board and Marketplace. A manager has to visit three tabs and synthesize them alone.

Make home a short, ranked list of this week's moves (lineup, waiver, plan ahead, trade idea), each with a one-line reason in both stats and stars. See mock A.

P1

Star scores never reach the lineup

Matchups → player favorability cards; home → start/sit

Malachi Fields (WR2) is "Celestially Challenged" at 61.6 and projects 5.4 points. Kalif Raymond sits on your bench at 7.6 points. Nothing connects the two. The start/sit card only ever compares a starter with a bench player at the same position, so it recommends the obvious (start Jonathan Taylor).

Run a lineup check over every slot, including FLEX and SUPER_FLEX. When stats and stars disagree, say so and let the λ dial break the tie, e.g. "Stats favor Raymond by 2.2 pts, stars favor Fields 62 to 50. At λ 0.35, start Raymond."

P1

Ten scores, no hierarchy

Every player and team surface

One player carries DraftScore, Spirit, Harmony, Wk Fav, VOR, projection (two kinds), and a 5-tier breakdown. Teams add Composite, Astral Favorability Index and Squad Harmony. All are 0–100 and none is marked as the one to act on. "DS" is never expanded on the board.

Show two numbers per player everywhere: Stats (this week's projection) and Stars (this week's favorability), plus one Oracle score that blends them by λ and drives every ranking. Move the rest into the player drawer.

P1

The λ dial is hidden on its own tab

Scoring Model

λ is the one control that expresses "how much do I trust the stars", and it reorders every list in the app. It sits below a decorative astrolabe, previews only Josh Allen (not available to you), and is duplicated in Settings.

Put λ in the header as a chip ("Oracle 0.35"). Opening it shows which of your moves change if you move it. See mock F.

P1

No path from advice to Sleeper

Waiver cards, Player Board, spotlight

"Recommended upgrade: +42.7 DS gain" has no button. The spotlight is labelled "read-only". The user has to switch apps and search for the player by hand.

Every move gets one primary action that opens the league in Sleeper, plus the player name ready to copy.

Superseded: the app is read-only by design, so the shipped version has no Sleeper action buttons. Each move names the players, slot and reason instead. The mock-ups below still show the original proposal.

P2

Colors contradict their own legends

Player Board Wk Fav pill; Matchups legend vs cards; standings badges

Cade Otton's "75 FAV" pill is red, the same red as "50 CHG". The Matchups legend marks Astrally Favored amber and Neutral grey; the cards render them green and amber with a warning triangle. BakchodLaunde (4-0, first place) wears a red "Discordant (At Risk)" badge beside its record.

One semantic scale used everywhere: green favored, amber neutral, red challenged. Use the accent hue only for brand. Show spirit tier on standings as a quiet secondary bar, never as a warning. See mock E.

P2

Repeated celestial text reads as fake

Matchups; player catalysts and risks; marketplace cards

Every matchup shows "Fire & Air" vs "Earth & Water" and "Jupiter Trine Mars (+14.2)". Every player lists "High volume red-zone target share" and "Tough division matchup concentration". These are constants in the code. The marketplace marks Fire vs Water "Aligned".

Derive these from each team's and player's actual traits, or remove them. Once a reader spots one repeated line, they stop trusting the whole page.

P2

Pages are very long and say things several times

All tabs, worst on phone

Home is 7 screens tall on desktop and 16 on a phone. Player Board is 12 and 27. Each waiver card is about 900 px and states the same swap four times: header chips, two side cards, a comparison table, then a quote and a footer.

One summary line per item with details on demand. Waiver cards fit in about 140 px (mock C). Paginate or virtualize the board and make its header sticky.

P2

Explainers sit on top of the content

Matchups, Dashboard, Scoring Model

Matchups opens with a four-factor explainer and a legend before the matchup. Every page's subtitle describes the software ("High-density mobile data grid with instant search…") instead of the user's task.

Lead with the answer. Move explanations behind an info button or into a single Oracle guide.

P2

Competitor rosters are a wall, not a tool

Home → League Draft Status & Competitor Rosters

Twelve cards and 180 rows, mostly red "Discordant" badges, with starters out of lineup order (a TE listed first). The data holds real trade leverage: BakchodLaunde and The Squeal Queens roster only 2 QBs in a superflex league; you roster 3.

Turn this into a Trades tab that ranks partners by need against your surplus. See mock D.

P2

Waiver suggestions ignore positional need

Marketplace

Four of five suggestions add a tight end. One drops a quarterback (Tua) in a superflex league to add a TE you would not start over Ferguson or Likely. The checkbox "Allow signed players to swap for free agents" is hard to parse.

Weight suggestions by lineup need and bye coverage. Rename the toggle "Suggest dropping players on an NFL roster".

P2

Draft-day features linger after the draft

Path of Ascension, DraftScore, "Slot N" labels, Ascension timeline

"15-round divination roadmap collapsed. Roster is fully ascended." still takes a panel on home in week 5. Standings rows lead with draft slot.

Switch modes when the draft is complete: hide draft panels, rename DraftScore to Oracle score, drop slot labels.

P3

Two navigation bars on desktop, four status badges

Global header

The top tab row and the phone bottom bar both show at 1440 px. The header carries a Live/Mock switch, a LIVE pill, a "LIVE SYNC" strip, and raw league and draft IDs.

Bottom bar on phones only. One sync indicator ("Synced 2 min ago"). Move Mock and the IDs into Settings.

P3

Phone layout overflows on the Player Board

Player Board at 390 px

The page is 415 px wide on a 390 px screen; the position chip row pushes it. Names truncate ("Michael M…", "Pat Freier…") while each card shows both an "AVAILABLE" and an "OPEN" chip.

Let the chip row scroll inside its own container, drop the duplicate status chip, give the name the full first line.

P3

Table details

Player Board table

Team cells wrap on some rows ("DAL / (14)") and not others. The bye number in parentheses has no label. "PROJ (SLP / NFL)" is two numbers that usually match.

Fixed column widths, "Bye 10" spelled out, one projection column with league scoring.

P3

Weight sliders say locked while they move

Scoring Model → Divination Tier Weights

Each weight has a slider and a second, redundant progress bar. The total reads "100% CANONICAL HARMONY (LOCKED)". Factor names here (Stadium Feng Shui, Jersey Gematria) differ from the Matchups factors (Spirit Baseline, Astral Transit).

One control per weight, a live total, and one vocabulary for factors across the app.

P3

Decoration takes the first screen of Scoring Model

Scoring Model

The animated astrolabe fills the viewport with badges like "SPEED 2.0x" and "<0.1ms" that mean nothing to a manager.

Shrink it to a header ornament, or use it as the λ dial itself.

03 · Structure

Four tabs, one dial

Reorganize around the weekly calls. The method (λ, weights, the encyclopedia) moves into one Oracle panel opened from the header chip.

05 · Copy

Keep the voice, add the plain label

The mystical names are the product's charm. Pair each one with what it does so a new user can act on it without the encyclopedia.

06 · Plan

Suggested order

Quick winsdays

  1. Fix the tier colors and the Matchups legend.
  2. Hide the bottom nav on desktop; collapse header status into one indicator.
  3. Fix the phone overflow on the Player Board.
  4. Rewrite subtitles and labels per the copy table.
  5. Hide draft panels once the draft is complete.
  6. Remove or derive the constant transit, element and catalyst text.

Core rework1–2 weeks

  1. Stats / Stars / Oracle as the only three player numbers.
  2. Lineup check across all slots, with λ as the tie-break.
  3. This Week home with ranked moves and Sleeper links.
  4. λ as a header chip with a live preview panel.
  5. Compact waiver cards with need-aware ranking.

New capabilitylater

  1. Trades tab from roster need and surplus.
  2. Real per-team element mix for matchup and trade copy.
  3. Bye planning that suggests pickups two to three weeks ahead.
